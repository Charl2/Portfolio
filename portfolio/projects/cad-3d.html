Context

From ECE Paris to Mexico: Two CAD Environments

My first exposure to 3D CAD came at ECE Paris, where I worked on hardware-adjacent projects including a connected health-tracking armband: modeling the enclosure, integrating electronics, and designing the physical form around sensor and battery constraints. No images from that phase, but it built the foundational thinking.

The work shown here comes from my semester abroad at UDLAP in Mexico, where I enrolled in a Rhino3D course taught entirely in Spanish. Rhino was new to me at the time. The curriculum covered form exploration, reverse engineering, additive manufacturing constraints, and technical precision modeling. Two of the final models were physically produced.

01 / Seating Design

Form, structure and ergonomics

Three chair designs produced through iterative modeling, each exploring a different structural approach and seating geometry. The exercise focused on translating form intent into clean NURBS surfaces, handling compound curves, leg joinery, and seat-to-back transitions in Rhino3D.

One of these designs was taken all the way to physical production: 3D printed at hand scale, a miniature model of the chair.

02 / Reverse Engineering

Inferring geometry from photographic reference

Three consumer products (a floor lamp, a Coca-Cola bottle, and a rubber duck) were reconstructed from photographs alone, without access to technical drawings or physical measurements. The exercise required decomposing each object into its constituent surfaces, inferring proportions from perspective cues, and rebuilding them with accurate topology in Rhino3D.

03 / Additive Manufacturing

Designed, printed and molded

This skull was designed for physical production from the start. The brief required satisfying FDM printing constraints: minimum wall thickness, self-supporting overhangs, and internal cavity geometry compatible with post-print molding. The digital model was sent to print, then physically molded.

Every surface decision was driven by what the printer and mold process could actually handle. Sculptural intent constrained by manufacturing reality.

04 / Precision Engineering

Technical recreation to engineering tolerance

The final exercise required recreating provided technical drawings at exact specifications, matching dimensions, radii, and surface continuity to engineering tolerances. This involved working with Rhino3D's constraint-driven tools, Boolean operations, and dimensionally-controlled curve networks.

The outcome demonstrated proficiency in translating 2D technical drawings into fully parametric 3D models ready for downstream manufacturing use.
